
                    On finding the pointer

    Once a screen gets large enough, the mouse pointer stops
    being a reliable landmark. It disappears between windows,
    it hides in a corner, and the eye has to go looking for it
    instead of getting on with the work. For some people that
    is a minor irritation. For others - anyone with reduced
    vision, or who struggles to hold the line while reading -
    it is the difference between using the machine and not.

    A crosshair solves it by making the pointer impossible to
    lose. Two lines across the whole screen, with a gap around
    the pointer itself, so it is never hidden by its own mark.

                    On following a line

    The reading line is the same idea turned toward text. One
    unbroken horizontal rule that follows the mouse down the
    page, like the ruler you used to lay under a sentence at
    school. You read faster, and you never skip a line.
